# Annulus: the style rulebook

Annulus is drawn by [STYLE.md](STYLE.md), Atlas Minor's rulebook, with one change: its test.

## The one test

> **Would a botanical engraver of about 1820 have drawn it this way?**

The references are the plates of transverse sections in the botanical and forestry books of about 1780 to 1850, and the wood specimens of the xylotheques of the same years: earnest drawings of the newest science, not old-looking ones.

## What that means here

- **Ink only, two line weights.** The fine line for the rings, the sap, the bark's fissures and all hatching; the heavy line for what matters most: the bark's outer edge, latewood where there is room for it, the edge of heavy sap, a frost crack, a fire's scar.
- **Hatching, never gradients.** The slice's shadow is parallel strokes outside it, away from the light (upper left). The sap is short radial strokes. A fire's char is cross-hatching.
- **Earlywood to latewood, pale to dark,** in steps, round each ring's loop from where its year began.
- **Signs, not pictures.** The sun is the astronomer's sign, a ring with a dot. Rain is fine ruled strokes. Decades are the dendrochronologist's pricked dots. No icons, no words on the slice.
- **The watercolour ink** washes the wood as a colourist tinted a printed plate: light washes multiplied into the paper, the heartwood a shade darker. The ink still does the work.
- **Fading ink, as STYLE.md says:** rings out of view hold 25 s, fade over 45 s to pencil, never below, and are inked again when turned back to.
- **One motion at a time.** Only the year's pen moves for attention. The ink coming back to a faded ring has no nib.
- **Out, always:** the same traps as STYLE.md. No bark texture photographs, no wood-grain gradients, no knots drawn for charm, no wobble.
