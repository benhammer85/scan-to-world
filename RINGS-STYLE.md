# Well Rounded: the style rulebook

Well Rounded (the tree-ring game; its working title was Annulus) is drawn by [STYLE.md](STYLE.md), Atlas Minor's rulebook, with one change: its test.

## The one test

> **Would a botanical engraver of about 1820 have drawn it this way?**

The references are the plates of transverse sections in the botanical and forestry books of about 1780 to 1850, the small habit drawings of the whole plant those plates set beside their details, and the wood specimens of the xylotheques of the same years: earnest drawings of the newest science, not old-looking ones.

## What that means here

- **Ink only, two line weights.** The fine line for the rings, the stroke being painted, the bark's fissures and all hatching; the heavy line for what matters most: the bark's outer edge, a fire's scar, the dead surface where the cambium died, a crack where a ring was left open, a thick limb's shaded edge. (The rings are all fine: a heavy latewood stretch on rings five years wide drew every ring as a dark spiral arc.)
- **Hatching, never gradients.** The slice's shadow is parallel strokes outside it, away from the light (upper left). A fire's char is cross-hatching. The wet stroke is radial hatching, close where the brush is wet and broken where it's dry. In the habit drawing, the hill and the crown's shaded side are hatched.
- **Earlywood to latewood, pale to dark,** in steps round each ring the way its stroke went. Where the stroke began and ended, its seam shows, as an ensō's does.
- **Signs and scenes, not words.** The sun is the astronomer's sign, a ring with a dot, rayed in a drought. Decades are the dendrochronologist's pricked dots. Where the next ring goes is a dotted pencil line, an engraver's construction line. The ink left in the brush is a drop at its tip. The weather is never written: it acts on the brush, and it happens in the habit drawing (clouds and ruled rain, heat shimmer and cracked ground, wind streaks, a ruled cold sky and hoar, smoke and tongues of flame). No icons, no words on the slice.
- **The habit drawing** is a small engraving of the whole oak on its hill: a short bole and heavy limbs in ink, foliage as an engraver touches it (scalloped leaf-masses, a few loops of leaf within, the side away from the light hatched), the hill's line heavy and hatched beneath. It grows with the rings and turns with the seasons.
- **Ink for what stays, watercolour for what changes** (the volcano's rule, VOLCANO-STYLE.md). With the watercolour ink the stroke in progress is a mottled wet wash, deepening as it goes and scratchy as the ink runs out, which dries into ink when the ring closes; finished wood is washed as a colourist tinted a printed plate, the heartwood a shade darker; in the habit drawing the leaves' season and the weather (blue-grey rain, warm fire, pale dry drought, a cold blue frost) are washes over the ink. The ink still does the work.
- **Fading ink, as STYLE.md says:** rings out of view (when you come close) hold 25 s, fade over 45 s to pencil, never below, and are inked again when back in view.
- **One motion at a time.** Only the brush moves for attention while a ring is painted. The ink coming back to a faded ring has no nib.
- **Out, always:** the same traps as STYLE.md. No bark texture photographs, no wood-grain gradients, no knots drawn for charm. Wobble only where a hand put it: the stroke's path is kept as drawn.
