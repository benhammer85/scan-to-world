# Well Rounded: the style rulebook

Well Rounded is drawn by [STYLE.md](STYLE.md), Atlas Minor's rulebook, with one change: its test.

## The one test

> **Would a botanical engraver of about 1820 have drawn it this way?**

The references are the plates of transverse sections in the botanical and forestry books of about 1780 to 1850, the small habit drawings of the whole plant those plates set beside their details, and the wood specimens of the xylotheques of the same years: earnest drawings of the newest science, not old-looking ones.

## What that means here

- **Ink only, two line weights.** The fine line for the rings, the line being drawn, the bark's fissures and all hatching; the heavy line for what matters most: the bark's outer edge, a fire's scar, the dead surface where the cambium died, a thick limb's shaded edge. (The rings are all fine: a heavy latewood stretch on rings five years wide drew every ring as a dark spiral arc.)
- **Every ring a smooth curve.** A ring is drawn as a smooth closed curve through its points, never a polygon, and its edge is the hand's line low-passed over some fifteen degrees: a shaky hand gives gentle undulations, as real rings are uneven, and never spikes. An engraver drew a section's rings as flowing lines, not a starburst.
- **Wounds lie along the ring, never across it.** Where wood died it is a soft dark band along its ring, hatched along its length and tapering at each end where the callus rolls over it; the rings after it close in round it. Never a radial wedge or a spoke from the pith.
- **Hatching, never gradients.** The slice's shadow is parallel strokes outside it, away from the light (upper left). A fire's char is cross-hatching. The wet line's wood is radial hatching from the bark to the edge, broken where the brush's wood is spent. In the habit drawing, the hill and the crown's shaded side are hatched.
- **Earlywood to latewood, pale to dark,** in steps round each ring the way its stroke went, and darker where the hand went slowly (the pace's only mark: it never changes the width). Where the stroke began and ended, its seam shows, as an ensō's does.
- **Signs and scenes, not words.** The sun is the astronomer's sign, a ring with a dot, rayed in a drought. Decades are the dendrochronologist's pricked dots. Where an even ring would land is a dotted pencil circle round the pith, an engraver's construction line (it may breathe, slowly, as a suggestion of pace). The wood left in the brush is a drop at the pen's tip; resting, the drop waits. The weather is never written: it acts on the line, and it happens in the habit drawing (clouds and ruled rain, heat shimmer and cracked ground, wind streaks, a ruled cold sky and hoar, smoke and tongues of flame). No icons, no words on the slice.
- **The habit drawing** is a small engraving of the whole oak on its hill: a short bole and heavy limbs in ink, foliage as an engraver touches it (scalloped leaf-masses, a few loops of leaf within, the side away from the light hatched), the hill's line heavy and hatched beneath. It grows with the rings and turns with the seasons.
- **Watercolour is the wood's own look** (the default; engraving is the alternative). The paint is simulated, never drawn as a picture of paint: water and pigment spread, mix by absorbance as paints do, bloom, granulate into the paper's tooth, and as a wash dries its pigment gathers at its edge (the coffee-ring effect): those dried rims are the rings. The traced line is disappearing ink, dark and wet at the pen and evaporating behind it; only the colour stays. The seasons colour each ring round its loop (sap green, gold, rust, madder, a winter violet), and the weather tints the year: rain cool and full, drought thin ochre, frost a pale blue, a fire's bloom vermilion and sepia. Wet-in-wet where a ring was begun while the last was damp, crisp where it had dried. Never a digital gradient. Ink for what stays, watercolour for what changes (the volcano's rule, VOLCANO-STYLE.md):
- **Fading ink, as STYLE.md says:** rings out of view (when you come close) hold 25 s, fade over 45 s to pencil, never below, and are inked again when back in view.
- **One motion at a time.** Only the pen moves for attention while a ring is drawn. The ink coming back to a faded ring has no nib.
- **Out, always:** the same traps as STYLE.md. No bark texture photographs, no wood-grain gradients, no knots drawn for charm. Wobble only where a hand put it, gentled as a pen's line is: the stroke's path, low-passed, never jittered or blotted by the program.
