# Well Rounded: the style rulebook

Well Rounded (the tree-ring game; its working title was Annulus) is calm watercolour, by the volcano's rule (VOLCANO-STYLE.md): **ink for what stays, watercolour for what changes**, with forgiveness and quiet as its own rules.

## The one test

> **Would a watercolourist sitting with a cross-section of oak, on a slow afternoon, have painted it this way?**

## What that means here

- **The paint is alive while it's wet.** Colours run into one another where both are wet, drift on a slow swirl (and downwind on a windy ring), feather a little into dry paper, gather at the drying edge into a darker rim, and settle into the paper's tooth (granulation). Never a flat fill, never a hard digital edge on wet paint.
- **Colours mix as paint mixes.** Pigments are optical densities that add: blue over gold goes green, red over green goes brown. Each ring runs the seasons the way its stroke went (sap green, gamboge, raw and burnt sienna, madder); the weather mixes its pigment in (indigo rain, ochre drought, viridian wind, cerulean frost, vermilion fire).
- **Disappearing ink.** Dry paint fades. The newest ring glows; the older ones pale to ghosts, a soft wash of their colours with a faint pencil line. In the time-lapse and the plate all the colour comes back.
- **Pencil for what stays.** The ring lines, the guide one steady half-mark outside the bark, the decade dots, the habit drawing's tree and hill: fine pencil and ink lines, never heavy. The bark is a soft sepia wash and its line.
- **Forgiveness, shown.** No red, no warnings, no failure states. A gap closes by the water wicking across it; a thin ring is just a thin ring.
- **Weather as scenes, not words.** The habit drawing shows what's coming as a little watercolour: clouds and rain, glare and shimmer, wind in the branches, a cold sky, smoke and soft flames on the slope. No icons, no words on the slice.
- **Quiet.** One thing moves for attention at a time: the brush and its wet stroke. The paper is warm and plain, with only a faint tooth.
