# Warm to the Touch: what it should feel like

Every change to the volcano game is held to this page. If something doesn't fit, it changes, or this page does, on purpose.

## The one test

> **Is it beautiful, calm, and mesmerising to watch: destruction as a slow transformation, not an event?**

The game was drifting towards realism: each effect true to nature (smoke on a wind, an umbrella cloud and its shadow, cracks, rafts, embers, sparks, haze, a veil, burps, bubbles, splatter) and the screen always busy with small things moving at different speeds. Realism adds detail; calm takes it away. What's mesmerising is a few large forms moving slowly and continuously, in a rhythm: a lava lamp, ink dropped in water, a time-lapse of a real flow.

## What we learn from the games that do it

- **One hero, and it's a shape growing.** *Prune*'s branches, *Sandspiel*'s patterns. Here it's the lava: tongues that creep, branch and pool, a glowing core cooling to dark. Anything that competes with it for the eye goes.
- **Destruction as colour and form, never debris.** *Gris* draws a catastrophe as a wash spreading over the page; *Ōkami* as a stroke of ink. A burst is a slow bloom of ink that spreads and settles, not particles flying.
- **Few colours, and one alive.** Paper, the ground's ink, and the lava, the one living colour. Everything else greys back.
- **No words while it's played.** *Journey*, *Flower* and *Monument Valley* teach through the world. The world, the pressure's bloom and the dotted aim carry it; words belong on the card and the plate.
- **Nothing pops.** In *Alto* and *Flower* nothing appears or vanishes at once. Everything comes in and goes out eased.
- **Rhythm.** *Tetris Effect* and *Flower* breathe: build, release, watch, quiet. Each release blooms and settles fully before the next.
- **The simulation serves the picture.** *Townscaper* hides its system; *From Dust*, the closest game to this one, showed its simulation and lost the calm. Tune for how it looks, not how true it is.
- **The result is the reward.** *Townscaper* and *Prune* leave you a picture you're proud of. Here, the plate.

## The medium

**Watercolour, over an ink map.** Watercolour is a fluid and behaves as lava does (it runs, pools, spreads into what's wet, and hardens as it dries), so the lava is drawn as paint, cooling as drying. The rule that keeps it from turning to mush: **ink for what stays, watercolour for what changes.** The coast, the contours, the vent's mark and the aim's dots are fine ink lines, the map's skeleton; everything alive (the lava, the pressure, a burst, cooling) is wash. It's the tradition of the hand-coloured maps of the 1700s. Watercolour must be beautiful: varied, never one flat colour, and showing the swirl of wet pigment while it's wet.

## Rules

1. **The lava is the hero.** Its shape is the first thing worked on and the last thing compromised.
2. **Washes, not simulations, for what the player reads.** The pressure is a pale wash blooming slowly round the vent, wider as the heat builds, gold at its edge when it's time: flat on the map, so it reads the same from every side, and still when nothing's happening. (Smoke drawn as puffs, then as engraved strokes, then as rings, each read worse.)
3. **Spectacle is rare and short.** A great moment (a burst, a moon gathering, a summit falling in) is held, then gone, and quiet again.
4. **One motion at a time.** While the lava runs, nothing else moves for attention.
5. **Subtract first.** Before adding an effect, ask what it replaces. If nothing, it probably doesn't belong.
6. **Ink, not light.** Paper doesn't shine: no reflections or highlights that slide with the view. Light comes from a fixed upper left, as maps draw it. Lava's own light is its heat: it reads stronger in the dark, never from a sun's glint.
7. **Haptics in place of sound.** A knock for a moment, a long low shudder for the brink; never a buzz for routine.
